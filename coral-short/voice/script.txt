Did you know that in sixteen fifty-eight, a sultan in the Maldives built a mosque out of the ocean floor?
Sultan Ibrahim Iskandar wanted a Friday mosque in Malé that would outlast him.
One problem. On an island this flat, there's no quarry, no mountain. The tallest thing around is a coconut palm.
So his builders looked down... at the reef.
Divers cut blocks of coral and hauled them back to the beach.
Here's the trick: fresh from the sea, coral is soft enough to carve like wood.
So they carved every block while it was wet, and the sun baked it hard as stone.
Too hard, as one apprentice found out.
Block by block, the walls slotted together like a giant puzzle.
Even the graves got coral headstones: pointed for men, rounded for women.
Seventeen years later, the same sultan added a white minaret next door.
And more than three hundred and fifty years on, the coral mosque is still standing.
