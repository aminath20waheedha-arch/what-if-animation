# "The Mosque Built From the Sea": Did-You-Know short, script v1 (2026-11-25)

Format: ~60 s vertical "Did you know" story short. 2D cartoon cards in a painted 3D Maldives world (three.js in
Remotion, from the claude-animated-shorts skill), narrated with a free neural voice, synthesized SFX plus an
original island score.

## Facts (checked before voicing)
- Hukuru Miskiy (the Old Friday Mosque), Malé, Maldives. Built under **Sultan Ibrahim Iskandar I** (ruled 1648-1687),
  **completed 1658**, on the site of an older Friday mosque.
  (Wikipedia "Hukuru Miskiy"; UNESCO World Heritage tentative list "Coral Stone Mosques of Maldives", 2013)
- Walls are **coral stone cut from the reef**: blocks dressed and fitted together with interlocking joints.
  (UNESCO tentative list entry: "intricately carved coral stone", "joinery of interlocking coral blocks")
- Freshly cut porites coral is **soft enough to carve and hardens as it dries** in the air.
  (UNESCO entry; Maldives Heritage Survey, coral stone building notes)
- The cemetery's carved coral headstones: **pointed tops for men, rounded tops for women**.
  (Wikipedia "Hukuru Miskiy")
- The **Munnaaru** (round white minaret next to the mosque) was added in **1675** by the same sultan:
  1675 - 1658 = **17 years later**. (Wikipedia "Hukuru Miskiy")
- 1658 to 2026 = **368 years**, so "more than three hundred and fifty years".
- Malé is flat: the whole Maldives averages about 1.5 m above sea level, so there is no quarry stone (the joke about the
  coconut palm being the tallest thing around is an exaggeration of a true fact).

## Voice-over (the text sent to TTS is voice/script.txt)
1. Did you know that in 1658, a sultan in the Maldives built a mosque out of the ocean floor?
2. Sultan Ibrahim Iskandar wanted a Friday mosque in Malé that would outlast him.
3. One problem. On an island this flat, there's no quarry, no mountain. The tallest thing around is a coconut palm.
4. So his builders looked down... at the reef.
5. Divers cut blocks of coral and hauled them back to the beach.
6. Here's the trick: fresh from the sea, coral is soft enough to carve like wood.
7. So they carved every block while it was wet, and the sun baked it hard as stone.
8. Too hard, as one apprentice found out.
9. Block by block, the walls slotted together like a giant puzzle.
10. Even the graves got coral headstones: pointed for men, rounded for women.
11. Seventeen years later, the same sultan added a white minaret next door.
12. And more than three hundred and fifty years on, the coral mosque is still standing.
13. So, be honest: would you have had the patience to build a mosque one coral block at a time? Tell me in the comments.
