## Sources
- The Rannamaari legend is recorded by Ibn Battuta, who lived in the Maldives in 1343-44: a jinn from the sea appeared
  each month as a ship filled with lamps; the islanders left a young girl in the idol temple by the shore; she was found
  dead in the morning. A Maghribi (Moroccan) traveller, Abu al-Barakat (al-Barbari), took her place and recited the
  Quran through the night; the demon did not return, and the king converted to Islam. (Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*)
- Traditional date of the conversion: 1153 CE (AH 548), under the king later called Sultan Muhammad al-Adil.
- Abu al-Barakat's tomb, the Medhu Ziyaaraiy, stands in Malé near the Hukuru Miskiy (Old Friday Mosque).
- Kept family-friendly: "By morning, she was gone." Scholars debate the legend; the video says "according to legend".
